Article summary

Abstract

Face recognition is commonly altered in Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), yet the underlying neural mechanisms remain poorly understood. This project uses biologically grounded convolutional neural networks to test how two physiologically motivated factors, excitatory/inhibitory (E/I) imbalance and excessive internal noise, affect learned face representations.

By systematically varying the E/I gain ratio across model layers that map onto the ventral visual stream (V1 → V2 → V4 → IT), the models show that increased excitation degrades classification accuracy and disrupts the representational similarity structure of face identities. Internal noise produces complementary but distinct impairments, providing a mechanistic account that links cellular-level E/I disruption to systems-level face-recognition deficits.

Question
Can E/I imbalance and internal neural noise explain weak face representations in ASD?
Approach
Pathology-inspired gain and noise manipulations in face-recognition CNNs, evaluated with accuracy and RSA.
Core result
Over-excitation weakens identity structure more strongly than inhibition-dominated conditions.
Results

Key findings

The main results connect model-level perturbations to measurable changes in face-identification performance and representational geometry.

01

E/I imbalance degrades recognition accuracy

Excitation-dominated gain settings systematically reduce face classification accuracy relative to balanced or inhibition-dominated conditions.

02

Face identity representations become weaker

Representational similarity analysis shows a reduced separation between within-person and between-person structure under elevated excitation.

03

Internal noise produces a distinct impairment

Noise flattens representational structure more uniformly, suggesting a mechanism that differs from selective E/I-driven disruption.

Code and data

Repository structure

The repository contains two complementary modeling pipelines, plus figure assets and citation metadata for the published article.

small-scale-custom-cnn/

Original TensorFlow/Keras pipeline trained on a compact 10-identity, 50-image face dataset.

  • Custom CNN architecture and training notebooks
  • Included cropped image sample and manifests
  • Baseline analyses for E/I and noise manipulations
large-scale-cornet/

CORnet-based PyTorch pipeline extending the study to 100 VGGFace2 identities with LFW evaluation.

  • Pathology injection for E/I gain and internal noise
  • Multi-run training, RSA extraction, and visualization
  • R scripts for statistical analyses and output figures
